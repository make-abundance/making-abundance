# The Pointing-Out Piece

> [!question] Open exploration
> This piece hasn't been written yet. What follows describes what it needs to be and what questions remain open.

## What This Needs to Be

A short experiential piece — 600-1000 words — in the style of Loch Kelly's pointing-out instructions. Where [[The Contraction]] names the dread, this piece guides the reader through one moment of actually setting it down.

Not an argument. Not an explanation. A guided experience on the page.

The reader has just recognized the contraction (Step 1). They know the feeling now. This piece says: "Good. You've noticed the thing you've been doing. Now — for thirty seconds — stop doing it. Here's how."

## What It Must Do

- **Guide, don't explain.** The reader should feel a shift in their body, not understand a concept. If they sit back from the screen and take a breath, it worked.
- **Be brief enough to finish.** The contraction is exhausting. A long piece about releasing it would be ironic. Get in, create the opening, get out.
- **Not require belief.** This isn't meditation instruction. It's closer to "notice your shoulders are up — now let them drop." Observable, physical, immediate.
- **Connect backward to The Contraction.** The contraction is the setup. This is the payoff. Without Step 1, the opening has no contrast.
- **Create hunger for Step 3.** The reader has now felt the opening alone. The natural next question: "What would this feel like with other people?" That leads to [[The Campfire]].

## What's Referenced in [[The Sequence]]

> Guide the reader through one moment of opening. Not just naming the contraction but actually releasing it, briefly, on the page. Let them feel the relief.
>
> Success looks like: The reader sits back from the screen, takes a breath, and feels something shift. Even briefly. Even slightly. That's enough.

## Open Questions

- Can a written piece actually create the opening, or does it require a facilitator (even an SI one)?
- What's the right voice? First person? Second person? The Contraction uses third-person observation ("It's the tightness in your jaw..."). Should this shift to direct address?
- Does the piece stand alone, or does it need to be read immediately after The Contraction?
- Is there a risk of the opening feeling trivial if it's this short? Or is brevity the point — "you can do this in thirty seconds, anytime"?
